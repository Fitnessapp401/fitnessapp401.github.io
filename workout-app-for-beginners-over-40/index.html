Choosing a workout app as a beginner over 40

Most fitness apps are made for people who already train. If you are over 40 and just getting started, here is what actually matters â€” and how Destination Health is built around it.

What to look for

  • A plan, not a library. Hundreds of workouts sound good, but beginners do better with one clear session for today.
  • It fits your week. You should be able to choose how many days and how many minutes you have.
  • It starts gently. The first weeks should teach the movements, not exhaust you.
  • It respects pain and old injuries. Sore knees or a stiff back are common after 40. The app should avoid exercises that load them.
  • It adjusts. If a session was too hard or too easy, the next one should change.
  • It is easy to follow. Clear pictures, plain words, and no jargon.
  • It works where you train. At home with no equipment, with a pair of dumbbells, or at the gym.

Why strength training matters after 40

From around middle age, most people slowly lose muscle and strength if they don't train it. Regular strength training helps you keep doing everyday things with ease â€” carrying shopping, climbing stairs, getting up from the floor â€” and it is one of the most commonly recommended types of exercise for healthy ageing. Two or three short sessions a week is a realistic start.

How Destination Health does it

Destination Health is a free iPhone app made for exactly this situation. You tell it your goals, how many days you can train, how long you have and where you train. You can also mention anything that hurts. It then builds a plan for each day of your week.

Each session starts with a short warm-up and shows every exercise with a picture and three simple tips. A timer with sounds counts down timed moves, so you don't need to watch the screen. After the session the app asks how it felt and adjusts the next one: harder or easier exercises, more or fewer sets, or a swap for anything that hurt.

On rest days it suggests an easy walk or a short stretch. There is no account to create, and your workout history stays on your phone.

A simple first week

  • Day 1: a short full-body session â€” squats to a chair, wall or knee push-ups, glute bridges.
  • Day 2: an easy 15-minute walk.
  • Day 3: the full-body session again, a little more confident.
  • Rest of the week: one more session if it felt good, and walks on the other days.

This is general guidance, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or haven't been active for a long time, check with your doctor before you start.
